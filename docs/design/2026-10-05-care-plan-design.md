# Monthly Care Plan — Design

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Implemented (backend + API). Clinical review of the rules and content is still outstanding -- see the last section.
**Superseded in part:** nutrition and exercise are no longer regenerated on every reading. They are written once per pregnancy week, with a code-computed progress summary and short per-reading advice -- see `2026-10-06-weekly-care-plan-design.md`. The monthly container, staff edits, allergies, statuses, hospital preferences, sources and guardrails described here still stand.
**Terminology:** "hospital" and "organization" mean the same thing in this document (`Organization` = one hospital).

## Context

A reading is scored by the risk model (`momcare_model`). This feature extends the risk engine: for every
patient it produces a **monthly Care Plan** containing nutrition guidance, physical-activity guidance,
medications and notes, and keeps the first two up to date as new readings arrive.

Reference: Neuro_RPM has a staff-written monthly `CarePlan` (one per patient per calendar month, statuses
in_progress / completed / finalized, free-text `recommendations`, free-text medication list). It has **no**
automatic nutrition or exercise engine, no patient-facing care plan, and its month exists for CCM billing.
We borrow the monthly-record idea and the status flow only. Billing, the calendar-month rule, and the
admin-configurable field designer are **not** carried over.

## Decisions (finalized)

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| 1 | One Care Plan per **Pregnancy** per **pregnancy month**. A month is **30 days counted from day 1 of the pregnancy** (`EDD − 280`). A patient who is onboarded at week 22 starts at plan 6; there are no empty earlier plans. |
| 2 | Day 1 and gestational age come from the EDD already captured at onboarding (`Pregnancy.lmp`/`edd`; onboarding rejects a pregnancy with neither). No gestational age → no plan month; the API says so rather than guessing. |
| 3 | Trimester cutoffs: 1st ≤ 13w6d, 2nd 14w0d–27w6d, 3rd ≥ 28w0d. Defined once, next to `calculate_gestational_age()`. |
| 4 | Four sections, shown to staff and (read-only) to the patient: **nutrition, exercise, medication, notes**. The plan response also shows **allergies and conditions** (read from the patient/pregnancy, never copied). |
| 5 | **Nutrition and exercise are generated by an LLM through OpenRouter, as two separate requests** (the existing `core/ai/openrouter_client`), on every new reading, from the patient's full current state. The model is the platform-wide `AIProviderConfig.current_model` (editable by the platform admin at `/api/platform-admin/ai-config/`), so one switch changes summaries and care plans together. Output is validated and filtered by code before anyone sees it. |
| 6 | **Medication is never generated.** Staff enter it (providers only). No AI text may mention medicines, supplements, doses or calorie/weight targets. |
| 7 | **Notes** are staff-written and **always visible to the patient** ("Notes for the patient"). Existing `MonitoringNote` is untouched and stays staff-only. |
| 8 | Staff can create, edit and **deactivate** (never hard-delete) items in all four sections. Patients cannot write anything. |
| 9 | Allergies/conditions: two new onboarding fields on `Patient` (`food_allergies`, `dietary_preference`); conditions are the existing yes/no/unknown factor fields on `Pregnancy`. Editing them inside the care plan writes through to those records. |
| 10 | Three statuses: `in_progress` → `reviewed` → `finalized`, with reopen. Status tracks staff review only; it never hides the plan from the patient. |
| 11 | **Tighten at once, loosen slowly** (see below). Enforced in code, not left to the model. |
| 12 | Doctor edits stay on **that patient's plan** as an adjustment layer and survive every regeneration. |
| 13 | Edits are logged automatically; **3 distinct staff members** (any hospital staff role) in the same hospital, region and trimester replacing/removing the same item produce a suggestion for the hospital admin. Approval makes it a hospital preference used in later prompts. Nothing crosses hospitals. |
| 14 | Frontend (Care Plan tab, patient mobile page) is out of scope; the backend provides the API and Postman docs. |

## New app

`momcare_platform/modules/pregnancy/care_plans/` (`app_label = "care_plans"`). It lives under `modules`
because it depends on vitals/risk (`modules → core` direction). It reuses `core/ai/openrouter_client.py`
and `core/common` (scoping, permissions, `calculate_gestational_age`, regions, `Deactivatable`).
Routes are mounted explicitly in `config/api_router.py`.

Region is derived from the hospital's country (`Organization.region`, the same population the risk model uses); there is no stored region field on plans. Age band comes from the patient's date of birth, falling back to the reading's `age`. Gestational age is
always derived from the EDD, never stored.

## Month and trimester calculation

```
day_index   = days since (EDD − 280)            # 0-based; None if no EDD
month_number = day_index // 30 + 1              # plans continue past 10 for post-term pregnancies
period_start = (EDD − 280) + (month_number − 1) * 30
trimester    = from gestational weeks (cutoffs above)
```

The plan is labelled with its month number, week range and trimester. A trimester boundary can fall inside a
plan; the generated content follows the **current** trimester, so advice changes at the boundary without
waiting for the next month.

A plan is created at the first reading of a new pregnancy month. If a month has no readings it has no plan;
`current-care-plan` then returns the latest plan flagged `is_current_month: false`.

## Generation pipeline

Triggered by a `post_save` hook on `RiskAssessment` (one row is written per reading), exactly like the
existing `core.ai.services.regenerate_for_new_reading`: it runs inline, inside the reading's transaction (so the
tenant/RLS context is intact), wrapped in a savepoint. Failures are caught and logged and **never fail or roll back
the reading**. Latency: a reading that triggers a regeneration waits for the model call (20 s timeout per request, two requests, one retry
only for *invalid* output, never for a transport failure); unchanged readings make no call. A sweep management command (same pattern as `refresh_ai_summaries`) is a safety net for anything
missed. Every reading is evaluated; the plan changes only when the state changes.

1. **Build the state** from: trimester, `final_risk_level`, a **category** for each vital (BP, heart rate,
   temperature, hemoglobin, glucose, stress score, physical-activity score — the five clinical categories come
   from `momcare_model/clinical_categories.py`; stress/activity use coarse bands defined in `state.py`), region,
   age band, food allergies, dietary preference and the pregnancy factors answered "yes". Vitals come from the
   **single latest reading**. A vital that is missing from that reading keeps the category the current plan was
   built on (so a row without hemoglobin cannot flip the plan and flip it back); a vital never recorded stays
   unknown, never guessed. Plans show the date of the reading they were built from. The patient's own doctor
   edits and the hospital's approved preferences are **not** part of the state -- they are passed to the model
   when it regenerates, but must not force a regeneration of an unchanged patient.
2. **Compute `state_key`** (hash of the categories above). If it equals the current version's key → **no
   call**, the plan is continued ("your condition is unchanged, keep following your plan").
3. **Tighten/loosen rule.** Severity order: risk level, then each vital category. If the new state is worse
   in *any* respect, or a structural field changed (trimester, allergies, dietary preference, factors, age
   band) → regenerate **immediately**. If it is only better → regenerate after it has held for **two
   consecutive readings**. In v1 both sections follow this rule together (the diet does not loosen early).
4. **Call OpenRouter twice -- once for nutrition, once for exercise** -- each with the state (categories and
   ages only — no names, ids, CNIC or contact details), that section's approved hospital preferences (top ~10
   relevant to region + trimester) and that section's adjustments as constraints ("this patient's doctor replaced
   oats with bread"). Model name is a setting. Each answer must be JSON matching its schema below, including
   `sources`.
5. **Validate each answer** (reject → retry once → that section's fallback):
   - JSON schema valid; every item has a standard English `item_key`; `sources` present (see below).
   - **Banned-content check:** no medication, tablet/capsule/pill, supplement, dose (mg/mcg/IU), calorie or
     weight-target wording.
   - **Allergy check:** items matching the patient's allergens are removed/rejected.
   - **Activity cap:** high risk (`final_risk_level`) → only the gentle-baseline activity set; enforced by
     code regardless of model output.
6. **Fallback, per section:** if OpenRouter fails, times out or that section's output stays invalid, store a
   version flagged `is_fallback` using a safe generic baseline defined in code, naming it as the source. The other
   section is unaffected. The patient never sees an error.
7. **Store** a new `CarePlanSectionVersion` per section (append-only) with the inputs and the triggering
   reading. New generated content that nobody has reviewed returns a reviewed/finalized plan to `in_progress`.
   A new month whose state equals last month's starts with a copy of last month's versions (no model call).

Every version carries the standard disclaimer and warning signs (severe headache, bleeding, reduced fetal
movement, blurred vision, chest pain → seek care).

### Length and sources

- **Short on purpose.** A plan is advice a nurse could hand over, not an essay: the prompt asks for at most
  300 words of nutrition and 120 of exercise (about 450 in all), with 4-5 meals, up to 6 foods to eat, up to 5 to
  avoid, one hydration line, up to 3 timing tips; 2-4 activities, up to 3 things to avoid, up to 4 stop signs.
  **No weekly rotation** (the earlier 7-day idea was what made plans long). In code: hard limits of 380 nutrition
  and 160 exercise words (an answer over them is rejected: one retry, then the fallback) and every list is
  trimmed to its limit. Counted words exclude item keys, slots and numbers.
- **Sources.** The prompt tells the model to base advice only on recognised authorities: first the national or
  regional health authority for the patient's region (national dietary and antenatal guidelines, the WHO
  regional office), then global bodies (WHO, FIGO, ACOG, RCOG, NICE), never social media, blogs, forums or
  commercial sites, and to leave out anything it is not confident those authorities support.
  **Limit, stated plainly:** the model has no internet access during this call and answers from its training,
  so this is an instruction the model is asked to follow, not something the system can check. The guarantee
  would come from a curated, clinician-approved reference pack of official recommendations (with citations)
  included in the prompt -- not built yet.

### Where she lives, and the fallback

- Both prompts name the **hospital's country** as well as the region ("Pakistan (Asia)") and ask for the everyday
  foods and customs of that country and respect for its common religious and cultural food norms. With the region
  alone ("Asia") a real test produced pork and East-Asian dishes for a Pakistani hospital. The country is where
  the patient lives, not who she is, so it is safe to send.
- The generic fallback is **plant-based on purpose** (it knows nothing about the patient, and a vegetarian or vegan
  must never be told to eat meat, fish, egg or dairy); her allergies are removed from it as from any plan.

### Two separate requests, two separate answers

Nutrition and exercise are different advice with different inputs, limits and failure handling, so each is its own
model request, its own validated answer, its own stored version and its own fallback. One section failing
validation falls back alone; the other is untouched. A regeneration is two requests, made one after the other.
The doctor's edits and the hospital's approved preferences are filtered per section too (food advice never reaches
the exercise request, or the other way round).

**Each section carries `sources` and a creation time.** `sources` is 1-3 short names of the authority or guidance
document the section says it is based on (a missing or empty list makes the answer invalid: retry, then
fallback; links, unfilled placeholders such as "[Country]" and any source whose name trips the banned wording are dropped). It is stored with the version and
returned with `generated_at` (when that version was created) on every section. The API marks it
`sources_verified: false`, because it is what the model *says* it relied on, from its training -- nothing checks
it against the real document, and a model can name a source wrongly. The generic fallback names MomCare's own
baseline plan as its source.

Nutrition answer:

```json
{
  "meals": [{"slot": "breakfast", "item_key": "paratha", "text": "Paratha with dahi"}],
  "foods_to_eat": [{"item_key": "spinach", "text": "..."}],
  "foods_to_avoid": [{"item_key": "raw_egg", "text": "..."}],
  "hydration": "...",
  "timing_tips": ["..."],
  "sources": ["WHO recommendations on antenatal care"]
}
```

Exercise answer:

```json
{
  "activities": [{"item_key": "walking", "text": "...", "duration_minutes": 20,
                  "frequency_per_week": 5, "intensity": "light"}],
  "avoid": [{"item_key": "heavy_lifting", "text": "..."}],
  "stop_signs": ["..."],
  "sources": ["WHO guidelines on physical activity"]
}
```

## Doctor edits

- Staff additions/edits/removals on nutrition and exercise are stored as `CarePlanAdjustment` rows — a layer
  that is always applied **on top of** the latest generated version. Regeneration never removes it, and a
  removed `item_key` is never suggested again for that patient.
- Adjustment text passes the same banned-content check (400 on violation). Allergen conflicts in a doctor's
  own edit produce a warning, not a block.
- **Next reading, condition unchanged:** no regeneration (edits are not part of the state); the patient sees the plan with the doctor's edits,
  labelled "reviewed by your provider". **Condition changed:** regenerate, with the adjustments passed to the
  model as constraints. Regenerating right after an edit is deliberately *not* done.
- Every edit also writes a `PlanCorrection` row automatically (no UI involved).

## Learning from edits (per hospital)

1. Each `PlanCorrection` is keyed by (`organization`, `region`, `trimester`, `section`, `item_key`) where the
   key is the **replaced/removed** item, not the replacement.
2. When **3 distinct staff members** have corrected the same key, a `HospitalPreference` is created with status
   `suggested`. Its `payload` holds the proposed guidance text and the replacements doctors used.
3. The hospital admin can approve (optionally rewording), reject, or later deactivate. Approved preferences
   are included in prompts (top ~10 relevant). Correction/preference text is passed to the model as **data,
   not instructions**, and passes the banned-content check before it is saved.
4. Rejected: no hospital preference; patients whose plans were already edited keep their edits; the same key
   is not re-suggested until 3 new distinct-doctor corrections arrive after the rejection.
5. `item_key` standardisation: the model is told to use one standard English key per food/exercise; a small
   synonym map in code folds common variants (oatmeal → oats); the admin can merge duplicate suggestions.
6. Never cross-hospital. Platform-level or cross-hospital learning is out of scope.

## Statuses and permissions

| Action | Who |
|---|---|
| Read plan (staff view) | provider, nurse, care manager, hospital admin (own hospital only) |
| Read own current plan (read-only) | the patient |
| Edit nutrition / exercise adjustments, notes, allergies & conditions | provider, nurse, care manager, hospital admin |
| Create/edit/deactivate medications | provider only |
| Mark `reviewed` | any of the staff roles above |
| `finalize`, `reopen` | provider, hospital admin |
| Preferences: list/approve/reject/reword/deactivate | hospital admin |
| `platform_admin` | no access (placeholder app stays empty) |

`finalized` plans are locked until reopened. Editing a `reviewed` plan returns it to `in_progress`.
Each transition stores who and when. Cross-tenant reads return **404, never 403**.

## API (explicit routes in `config/api_router.py`, prefix `/api/`)

| Endpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `GET /care-plans/?pregnancy=` | List plans for a pregnancy |
| `GET /care-plans/{id}/` | One plan: header (month, weeks, trimester, status), nutrition, exercise, medications, notes, allergies and conditions — **everything in one response** |
| `GET /pregnancies/{id}/current-care-plan/` | Current plan for staff and the patient (read-only for patients) |
| `GET /pregnancies/{id}/current-care-plan/nutrition/` · `…/exercise/` | The current plan's nutrition or exercise alone (content with staff edits applied, `sources`, `sources_verified`, `generated_at`, label, status), for staff and the patient. There is deliberately no by-plan-id section endpoint: an older month is read through `GET /care-plans/{id}/`, whose `nutrition` and `exercise` blocks are already separate. |
| `POST /care-plans/{id}/adjustments/`, `PATCH`/`DELETE …/adjustments/{adj_id}/` | Add / edit / deactivate nutrition or exercise items (`DELETE` = deactivate) |
| `POST /care-plans/{id}/medications/`, `PATCH`/`DELETE …/medications/{med_id}/` | Provider-only CRUD (deactivate) |
| `POST /care-plans/{id}/notes/`, `PATCH`/`DELETE …/notes/{note_id}/` | Staff CRUD (deactivate) |
| `PATCH /care-plans/{id}/allergies-conditions/` | Writes through to `Patient`/`Pregnancy`; triggers regeneration |
| `POST /care-plans/{id}/review/`, `/finalize/`, `/reopen/` | Status transitions |
| `GET /care-plan-preferences/?status=` | Admin: suggestions and approved preferences |
| `POST /care-plan-preferences/{id}/approve/`, `/reject/`, `/deactivate/`; `PATCH …/{id}/` | Admin decisions / rewording |
| `GET /patients/?workflow=care_plan_review` | Existing patient list: this month's plan is still `in_progress` (query param, no standing endpoint) |
| `GET /patients/?workflow=care_plan_missing` | Existing patient list: no plan this month (no readings yet, or no EDD) |
| `GET /patients/dashboard-kpis/` (existing) | Gains `workflow.care_plan_review` and `workflow.care_plan_missing` counts (same conditions as the list filters, so they always agree) |

Reads are one aggregate call; writes are per-section because permissions and validation differ.
Pagination uses the standard `DefaultPagination` envelope. The Postman collection is updated with plain CRUD
names (Create/Get/List/Update/Delete).

## Testing

API-level integration tests on real Postgres, nothing mocked except the OpenRouter call (already patched
globally in `conftest.py`); pure-function tests for month/trimester maths, `state_key`, the tighten/loosen
rule, the banned-content and allergy checks, and `item_key` normalisation. Security tests are proven by
fault injection: cross-tenant 404 on every endpoint, patient cannot write, nurse cannot write medication,
no medicine/supplement text can be stored or returned, adjustments survive regeneration, a removed item is
not re-suggested, preferences never leak across hospitals. RLS is checked with `scripts/verify_rls.py`.
`uv run lint-imports` is part of standard verification.

## Build order

1. Models, migrations, RLS, month/trimester maths, statuses, read endpoints.
2. Generation pipeline: state/`state_key`, tighten/loosen, OpenRouter call, validation, fallback, versions.
3. Staff editing: adjustments, medications, notes, allergies/conditions write-through, onboarding fields.
4. Corrections log, suggestions, hospital preferences and admin endpoints.
5. Postman collection and docs.

## Not in scope

Frontend screens; billing; customizable care-plan form designer; medication catalog or any AI medication
text; emails (in-portal only); cross-hospital learning; model fine-tuning; languages beyond English;
calorie/weight targets.

## Open items

- None blocking. Demo data uses Asia patients; the model comes from the existing AI config; the generation
  hook is the existing `RiskAssessment` post-save pattern.
- Care plans use their own `max_tokens` (1500) and a 20 s timeout; the config's `max_words` cap applies to summaries only. `core/ai/openrouter_client.generate()` gained an optional `timeout` argument (default unchanged at 10 s).
- `manage.py sweep_care_plans` is the safety net for any reading whose plan was not evaluated; schedule it (e.g. every 15 minutes) next to `escalate_alerts`.
- The five vital category cutoffs reuse `clinical_categories.py`; the stress/activity bands, the severity ranking, the two-reading hold, the banned-word list and the activity caps by risk (high: light, at most 15 min, gentle set only; medium: light, at most 30 min; low: moderate, at most 45 min) are product decisions awaiting clinical review.
- A small model (e.g. Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite) follows strict JSON and safety instructions less reliably;
  code validation catches failures and the platform admin can switch to a larger model without code changes.
- **Clinical review required.** The vital category cutoffs, trimester cutoffs, safety rules and the activity
  baseline are **not reviewed by an obstetrician or dietitian**. LLM output has no pre-approved content.
  This feature must be labelled unreviewed in code and docs and must not be described as ready for real
  patients until a clinician has reviewed the rules and sample plans.
